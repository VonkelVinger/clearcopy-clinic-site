Editorial practice

How I use AI

I use AI where it can make editorial work more systematic or useful. I make the editorial judgements and remain responsible for the work I deliver.

AI as an editorial tool

Depending on the brief, AI can help identify inconsistencies, compare versions, organise material, suggest questions or explore alternative wording. Its output needs checking against the text, the sources and the purpose of the work.

AI output is not an authoritative source. I do not treat a fluent answer as evidence, and I check factual claims and references where that is part of the agreed brief.

Your project and restrictions

Tell me about confidentiality, publisher, employer or institutional requirements before work begins. We can agree what assistance is appropriate, which material may be processed and any disclosure needed. I do not submit restricted client material to an AI service without agreement.

Public tools and human work

The Article Clinic and Headline Clinic pilots generate automated coaching through an AI service. Their output has not been personally reviewed by me. The book self-check uses fixed rules in your browser and does not use AI. A commissioned editorial assessment is a separate service in which I read the supplied material.

Editorial responsibility

My aim is clearer, stronger and more trustworthy writing. The author’s meaning, evidence and voice guide the work. For academic work, the rules of the relevant institution or assessment apply.

Read the privacy policy and academic integrity policy, or ask me about your project.
